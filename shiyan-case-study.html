01 — The Problem

Three parties. Three broken loops.

The beauty nail and lash industry in China runs on trust, aesthetics and word-of-mouth — yet the discovery, booking and retention experience is fractured at every touchpoint.

Consumer (C-side)
The decision paralysis
Consumers find styles they love but can't visualise the result on their own hands or eyes. High decision cost, low conversion. Finding a trusted nearby artist is a separate friction entirely.
Micro-salon / Artist (B-side)
The one-person bottleneck
Independent nail artists and small salon owners are sole operators. They do the craft, manage bookings, create content, and chase retention — all alone. Marketing is the first thing that gets dropped.
Platform (our side)
The monetisation gap
Existing platforms match supply and demand but don't create ongoing value. No AI differentiation, no content infrastructure, no retention engine — commoditised from day one.
02 — Strategic Insight

The real product isn't
the nail art.

Core Insight "Beauty decisions are identity decisions. Reducing the risk of getting it wrong is more powerful than showing how good it could look."
Insight 01 — Confidence Gap
The primary barrier to booking isn't price or proximity — it's uncertainty about outcome. AI try-on removes that barrier at the exact moment of desire.
Insight 02 — Creator Economy Meets Beauty
Independent nail artists have creative capital and loyal micro-audiences but no infrastructure. They don't need more platforms — they need operational leverage.
Insight 03 — The Retention Opportunity
Post-service drop-off is the industry's silent revenue leak. Automated, personalised follow-up at day 1, 3, and 7 can double repurchase rates with near-zero marginal cost.
03 — Strategic Framework

One platform. Three flywheels.

Rather than building a two-sided marketplace, we designed a three-party value network where each participant's activity strengthens the others. AI is the infrastructure — not the product.

01
AI Try-On → Conversion Engine
Virtual nail and lash try-on at the moment of discovery. Converts passive browsing into active intent — and links directly to bookable artists. See it. Want it. Book it.
C-side flywheel
02
AI Content Factory → Acquisition
One-click generation of Xiaohongshu copy, Douyin scripts and promotional assets. What previously took a full-time social team now takes 60 seconds. Artists become content machines.
B-side flywheel
03
AI CRM → Retention Loop
Automated WeChat follow-up sequences at day 1, 3 and 7 post-service. Personalised by name, style and occasion. One artist, hundreds of clients — all feeling remembered.
Platform flywheel
04 — Go-to-Market Execution

90 days. Three phases.

1
Days 1–15: Lock the seed network
Sign the anchor chain salon. Recruit 3–5 independent Shenzhen nail artists on zero-fee, commission-only terms. Commission the MVP WeChat mini-programme focusing on AI nail try-on and basic booking.
Key asset
Anchor chain salon provides initial style library and first customer pool. Independent artists provide creative diversity and personal brand equity.
2
Days 16–30: Launch the AI stack
Deploy AI try-on, B-side content generator (10 localised Shenzhen templates), and enterprise WeChat CRM sequences. Chain salon becomes the live proof-of-concept for retention uplift.
Key metric
Target: 30% repurchase rate improvement at chain salon within 60 days. This becomes the sales case for broader merchant acquisition.
3
Days 31–90: Grow and validate
Launch "Shenzhen Beauty Lab" content matrix on Xiaohongshu and Douyin — AI-generated, artist-branded, all linking back to the mini-programme. Scale to 10–15 independent artists.
Key metric
Try-on to booking conversion rate. Content-driven traffic to mini-programme signups. Artist retention and monthly style submission rate.
05 — Business Model

Revenue that compounds.

Transaction Commission
15–20% on every platform-originated booking. The primary engine in Phase 1 — cash flow positive before SaaS subscriptions kick in.
Phase 1 Priority
SaaS Subscription
¥299/month for advanced AI content generation and CRM beyond free tier. Merchants who see value self-convert. No hard sell required.
Phase 2 Growth
Style Licensing Network
Independent artists license original styles to chain salons. Platform takes 5–10% of each cross-store transaction. Passive income for artists; competitive edge for salons.
Moat Builder
Managed Content Service
Full AI content and CRM management for merchants who don't want to operate the tools themselves. Higher margin, deeper lock-in, referral-worthy results.
High Margin
06 — Competitive Moat

Why this is hard to copy.

The style network effect
Each artist contributes one original style per month. Over time, the platform accumulates a proprietary, exclusively-licensed style library that no competitor can replicate without rebuilding the entire artist network. Data compounds; the moat deepens automatically.
Try-on to transaction in one flow
Other platforms show try-on as a feature. We designed it as the entry point to the entire commerce loop — try, discover artist, book, pay, get followed up with, return. The experience is the product.
The infrastructure play
Content generation and customer retention tools that were previously enterprise-only are now available to a one-person nail studio. We become the operating system for their business — not a marketing channel they can walk away from.
"The hardest part wasn't the AI strategy — it was designing a business model where all three parties win at the same time."
Strategist's Reflection
What I learned
Multi-sided platform strategy requires you to resist the urge to optimise for one party. Every feature decision is a negotiation between three sets of incentives.
The AI angle
AI isn't the differentiator here — the strategic sequencing is. AI try-on creates demand; AI content scales supply; AI CRM captures value. Each layer enables the next.
What I'd push further
User research with actual independent nail artists in Shenzhen to stress-test the "one style per month" commitment. The hypothesis is sound; the behaviour needs validating.
