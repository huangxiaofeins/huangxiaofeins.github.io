01 — The Brief

A union fighting for its members.
But is that the right battle?

The National Tertiary Education Union (NTEU) commissioned a campaign to raise public awareness of two issues in Australian higher education: the gender pay gap and the over-reliance on casualised teaching staff. With limited funding and around 30,000 members, NTEU needed to punch far above its weight to shift public opinion and pressure university management.

The initial brief positioned this as a labour rights story: unfair wages, insecure contracts, workers being exploited. True — but not compelling enough to break through to people outside the sector.

What the brief said
A wage justice and job security problem
Female staff earn significantly less than male counterparts. Casualised staff — over 43% of the university workforce — work full teaching loads without entitlements: no sick leave, no annual leave, no job security. Universities exploit this model to reduce costs.
What the data revealed
A structural incentive problem with a hidden victim
Casualised staff face a unique threat: if enrolments drop in their course, they get cut. This creates a perverse incentive — some staff deliberately fail students to force re-enrolments. The real victim isn't just the worker. It's the student. And that changes everything about how we campaign.
02 — Strategic Insight

Follow the incentive.
Find the real story.

I went beyond the brief to research how casualised employment actually functions inside universities. What I found was a chain of systemic pressure that no one was talking about publicly.

1
Universities hire casualised staff to cut costs
Casual staff can't be used as references for bank loans or visas. Many enter through informal channels — internal referrals from existing staff. Universities tolerate this because casuals cost significantly less than permanent hires, inflating institutional profit margins.
2
But casuals are measured on enrolment numbers
Despite their precarious status, casualised staff face performance pressure: if the number of students enrolling in their course declines year-on-year, they face redundancy. No enrolments, no contract renewal.
3
This creates a dangerous incentive loop
To protect their jobs, some casualised staff deliberately mark students down — issuing failing grades to force re-enrolments in the following year. Students who should pass are failed. Education quality is directly compromised by a structural employment model.
The hidden insight
4
The "bad money drives out good" effect
Universities that tolerate underqualified casuals while ignoring performance issues create an environment where capable staff are disadvantaged and bad actors are incentivised to stay. Academic standards erode. Student experience deteriorates. Trust in institutions declines.
Core Strategic Insight "This isn't just a labour rights story. Casualisation is a student rights story. When staff job security is precarious, student grade integrity is compromised. The system isn't just unfair to workers — it's structurally rigged against the people it's supposed to serve."
03 — The Reframe

Change who the
audience cares about.

The original framing asked the public to sympathise with workers they didn't know. The reframe made every student, parent, and taxpayer a stakeholder in the outcome. This fundamentally shifts the campaign's emotional pull and policy leverage.

Original frame
"Casualised staff deserve better pay and job security."
Audience: people who already care about labour rights. A passionate but limited base. Easily dismissed as self-interest by universities and government.
Strategic reframe
"Casualisation is failing students — and corrupting the integrity of Australian higher education."
Audience: every student, every parent, every employer who hires graduates. The stakes are now universal. Universities can no longer dismiss this as an internal labour dispute.
04 — Campaign Strategy

One campaign.
Three pressure points.

Rather than a single ask, the campaign was structured to build pressure across three audiences simultaneously — using different messages, tones, and channels for each, all unified under the #Secure&Equal hashtag.

01
Public Outrage → Media & Social
Make the issue impossible to ignore. Memes and shareable content put the contradiction of casualisation into language that young university workers and students can share without feeling like activists. The skeleton meme — still waiting for the gender pay gap to be fixed — was deliberately irreverent: it makes the point while lowering the barrier to share.
Awareness
02
Institutional Pressure → Media Releases
Two strategically timed releases: the first at launch to frame the problem with data (WGEA gender pay gap data, casualisation statistics); the second five days in to maintain momentum with participant stories. Targeting 7NEWS, 9NEWS, ABC, and radio (KIIS 106.5, Radio Focus) to reach beyond the sector echo chamber.
Earned Media
03
Community Mobilisation → Hashtag Relay
The #Secure&Equal hashtag relay — launched on Employee Rights Day (Feb 13) — invited affected workers to share their stories. A branded T-shirt incentive was offered to selected participants. This created a story supply chain: real voices, real names, real consequences. Far more persuasive than institutional statements alone.
Community
05 — Tactics & Execution

What we actually made.

Each tactic was mapped to a specific audience and moment in the campaign arc — not just a list of deliverables, but a coordinated sequence designed to build and sustain public attention over three weeks.

Tactic 01
Meme Series — Shareable outrage
Four memes adapting familiar formats (Distracted Boyfriend, Woman Yelling at Cat, Medieval Skeleton) to make gender pay gap and casualisation absurdities instantly legible to a young, social-media-native audience. Designed to spread without requiring context.
Target: Young university workers & students · Instagram / Twitter
Tactic 02 — Phoebe's contribution
Media Releases — Two-wave news strategy
Release 1 (Feb 13, Employee Rights Day): Campaign launch, data-led framing of the crisis. Release 2 (Feb 18): Sustain momentum with participant story — "As a casual, I went through many unjust experiences." Each release accompanied by 5 images calibrated for social and broadcast media separately.
Target: Journalists at 7NEWS, 9NEWS, ABC · Radio: KIIS 106.5, Focus
Tactic 03
Campaign Posters — Visual identity
"Say YES To An End of Gender Pay Gap" and "Say YES To An End of Casualisation" — direct, affirmative, designed for physical and digital distribution. The positive framing ("Say YES") was deliberate: it invites action rather than protest, making the ask feel achievable.
Target: Current university staff · On-campus & social distribution
Tactic 04
#Secure&Equal Hashtag Relay — UGC engine
Instagram story relay using the "Add Yours" sticker — seeded with NTEU's own content, designed for organic amplification. T-shirt incentive for selected participants drove initial engagement. Ran simultaneously with Twitter and Facebook campaign content to ensure cross-platform reach.
Target: Social-active university workers · Instagram / Twitter / Facebook
